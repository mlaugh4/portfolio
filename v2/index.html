Odin

Project overview

Context

Indeed’s legacy business (hosted job advertising) asymptoted in 2023. In an attempt to reignite revenue growth, SLT formed a tiger team to build an agentic recruiting and screening product for enterprise employers.

Product

Agentic recruiting assistant that uses screening criteria to intelligently find, screen, and summarize talent, as well as distribute spend efficiently across jobs.

Key challenge

Finding the goldilocks optimal input per job to maximize trust and accuracy, while minimizing legal and social risk. Balancing conflicting interests between buyers (HR leader) and daily users that stand to be replaced (Recruiters).

Outcome

Led team of 8 designers from concept through 3 pilot phases, and full deployment in January 2026 (currently multiple $1mm contracts). Navigated complex legal, RAI, and multi-constituent problem space.

Turnkey setup

Our first challenge was to address pressure to keep onboarding lightweight, but provide just enough control and transparency to ingrain trust. We learned in generative that XL employers live in their ATS and have little tolerance for outside tooling and elaborate config.

Agent customization

Employers that pay for “Headhunter” are invited to tune the agent’s discovery and screening criteria. Research found that investment in this phase predicts higher chance of hiring outcomes and positive qualitative feedback, yet

Mapping key AI value drivers

90% of Odin’s value was in the relevance of candidates delivered. This was an iceberg product: Super simple AI with highly refined business logic. Given these employers are largely embedded in their ATS, a big part of my job was working with our Head of Machine Learning to define two key moments: Generating default screening criteria (see prior slide), and delivering wow-level candidates with the right proof of fit.

Human-in-the-loop when it matters

Trust is the dominant term in the AI adoption function. This is particularly salient in high-stakes applications like hiring. While we found employers were quick to outsource screening and sourcing functions, we found human intervention was necessary for many tasks (e.g. budget management and interview scheduling).

Communicating “fit”

The candidate summary page is where employers spend 95% of their time, as they are delivered to their ATS; prepended to the resume. It includes three pages: The summary (pictured), resume, and screening interview summary. The information presentation rides the balance between extensive UXR and legal guidance.

AI slop butterknife

Hiring is a bit of an AI arms race. Part of Odin’s value proposition is going beyond the AI-gen resume (a problem we also help create) to identify real humans that actually have required skills and certifications. This flow was additive and took careful consideration to prevent significant volume decline.

PrepTrack

Context

Volunteer UX for org out of Cambridge University that simplifies PreP adherence. Provided low-cost UX suggestions across end-to-end, built a design system, and offered some vision flows. The team hadn’t had any UX input and the application was very confusing. And, frankly, ugly.

Product

Mobile app to help people manage HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) medication. It recorded pill intake and sex timing, provided dosage reminders, tracked protection status for daily or event-based PrEP (aligned with UK guidelines), and stored all data privately on the user's device.

Project overview

Context

VR and AR was the hot emerging tech when I was in grad school. In my final year, I linked up with two PhD students to build a system for interventional cardiologists to educate and prepare for complex procedures.

Product

In close collaboration with practitioners, we built a few experiences: VR viewer with hybrid interaction model (more later), AR viewer, and a desktop model management portal.

Key challenges

  1. Virtual reality is a big shift from current visualization tech, and it’s introduction to the hospital requires a deep understanding of surrounding players and influences. We used ‘fly on the wall’ observation and contextual inquiry to better understand and document workflows around medical imaging and patient care protocols
  2. One byproduct of this big shift is that novelty of interacting with volumetric data. You can’t naturally use a keyboard and mouse in VR, so we spent extensive time in the lab testing various input models.

Outcome

Partnership and seed money from the University of Washington. Features in Seattle Times, Geekwire, and KING5 news. Pilots at 5 major research hospitals. vr workshop at largest radiology conference in the world. Startup has gone through several rounds and evolved into Dopl.
